# 05 — Civic Action ("what you can do")

**Purpose:** Give the user an emotional and visual outlet for the "okay, but what can I do
about it?" question. Depicts the citizen ↔ government feedback loop as something a person
can actually act on. (Thread T3 in PLAN.md.)

**Where it's used:** a "What you can do" section on the receipt / detail pages, a CTA band,
the civic-action layer. Echoes the *cyberfolk* idea: residents linked into governance via
real-time feedback.

## Prompt

> A flat geometric illustration of a civic feedback loop: on one side a simple, diverse
> resident; on the other a rounded "government / agency" building node; between them a
> two-way set of thin indigo ribbons forming a clear loop — one direction labeled by small
> icons for "comment at a hearing" (a speech bubble / podium), "contact your agency" (an
> envelope), and "vote on a measure" (a ballot box); the return direction shows services
> flowing back (book, road, leaf). Calm, empowering, daylight tone — the person looks
> capable, not helpless. The loop is the hero of the composition. Generous whitespace.
>

## Variations

- **A — Calendar motif:** add a small calendar/clock element to evoke "next budget hearing
  / reauthorization date" (ties to the civic-calendar feature).
- **B — Three-step strip:** three rounded tiles — "See where it goes → Find the lever →
  Act" — for an instructional band.
- **C — Share motif:** a receipt being shared outward to other people (share-the-receipt as
  civic action).

Aspect ratios: `16:9` (CTA band), `1:1` (section / card), `3:2` (explainer).

## Acceptance

- ✅ The two-way loop (citizen → government → services → citizen) is obvious.
- ✅ The action icons (comment, contact, vote) are distinguishable.
- ✅ Empowering, non-partisan, non-protest tone; no flags/politicians.
- ❌ Reject if it reads as protest/anger, depicts helplessness, or shows party symbolism.
